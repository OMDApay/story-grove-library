BuilderKid22

"You're SO bad at this game, just quit already," Ziad typed into the group chat during his online building game, laughing at his own screen.

A little English

"You're SO bad at this game, just quit already," Ziad typed into the group chat during his online building game, laughing at his own screen. His older sister, Rimas, glanced over from her desk. "Who are you talking to like that?" "Just some random kid in my game," said Ziad, shrugging. "He kept messing up our tower. It's not a big deal, it's just typing." Rimas set down her pencil. "Would you say that to his actual face, if he were standing right here?" Ziad paused, the question catching him off guard. "I don't know. Probably not that exact way." "Why not?" asked Rimas. "Because it would feel meaner, saying it out loud to someone's actual face," Ziad admitted slowly. "But you did say it," said Rimas. "Just through a screen instead of your mouth. Does typing it make the words hurt him any less on the other end?" Ziad hadn't really considered that before. "I guess he still reads it the same way, whether I typed it or said it." "That's actually a rule a lot of people use online," said Rimas. "Before posting or typing something, ask yourself: would I say this to their face? If the answer's no, that's usually a sign not to send it." Ziad looked back at his screen, where the other player, someone named "BuilderKid22," had gone quiet after his comment. "I feel kind of bad now," Ziad admitted. "I don't even know if BuilderKid22 is a kid my age or someone way younger." "That's another thing about online stuff," said Rimas. "You genuinely don't know who's actually behind the screen. Could be someone your age. Could be someone much younger, having a rough day already." Ziad typed a new message: "Sorry, that was mean. My bad." He waited, unsure if the other player would even respond. A reply came a moment later: "its ok, im only 6, my brother usually helps me build but hes not here today." Ziad felt his stomach drop slightly. "He's six," he told Rimas. "I was actually kind of harsh to a six-year-old." "Now you know," said Rimas. "What do you want to do with that information?" Ziad thought for a moment, then typed: "want help building? I'm actually pretty good at towers." A string of excited emoji replies came back almost instantly. For the next twenty minutes, Ziad helped BuilderKid22 construct a detailed tower, tolerantly explaining which blocks worked best at the base. That evening, Ziad told his sister how it had gone. "He was actually really happy I helped instead of just leaving after apologizing," he said. "It's weird, I feel better than I did before any of this happened." "That's what happens when you treat someone online the way you'd treat them in person," said Rimas. "Being a decent digital citizen isn't complicated. It's just the regular kind of decent, applied to a screen." "Digital citizen," repeated Ziad, testing the phrase. "I like that. I'm going to try to actually be one from now on."
