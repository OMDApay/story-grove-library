The Shadow Under the Bed

Every night at exactly nine o'clock, something scratched under Karim's bed.

A little English

Simple past tense; past habitual actions ("every night"); multi-night sequencing (the next night, the following evening); sensory description; dialogue expressing reasoning and hypothesis-testing.

Every night at exactly nine o'clock, something scratched under Karim's bed. Scritch. Scratch. Scritch. Karim froze under his blanket, his eyes wide open in the darkness. His night-light cast a soft yellow circle on the ceiling, but it did nothing to reach the black shadow stretching beneath his bed frame. "MOM!" he yelled. Footsteps thumped down the hallway. His mother pushed open the door, flipping on the light. "What's wrong, habibi?" "Something's under my bed," Karim whispered, pointing a shaking finger. "It scratches. Every single night." His mother sighed gently and sat on the edge of the mattress. "I checked last night. And the night before that. Remember?" "Maybe it hides when you look," said Karim. His mother thought for a moment, then smiled. "Tomorrow, let's try something different. Instead of me checking for you, what if we investigate together, like detectives?" Karim sat up slightly. "Detectives?" "Real detectives don't just believe the scariest explanation," said his mother. "They gather clues first." The next night, Karim's mother handed him a small notebook and a pencil. "Tonight, when you hear the sound, don't shout. Write down exactly what you notice. What does it sound like? When does it happen? How long does it last?" Karim nodded nervously, gripping his pencil tightly under the blanket. At nine o'clock, the scratching began again. Scritch. Scratch. Scritch. Karim's hand trembled, but he forced himself to write: "Sounds scratchy. Comes from the corner, near the window. Stops after ten seconds." "Near the window," he whispered to himself. "Not under the bed at all." The following evening, Karim's mother brought a flashlight. Together, they knelt beside the window, not the bed. Karim aimed the light toward the corner where the sound seemed to come from. There, on the outside windowsill, a small tree branch scraped gently against the glass every time the night wind blew. "It's just the old oak tree," said Karim's mother, laughing softly with relief. "The wind pushes that branch against your window." Karim stared at the branch, feeling a strange mixture of embarrassment and enormous relief. "All this time, I thought it was under my bed. But I never actually looked at the right place." "That happens more than you'd think," said his mother. "Fear tells us the scariest story before we check the facts. Real detectives — and brave kids — check the facts first." The next morning, Karim asked his father to trim the branch so it would no longer touch the window. That night, for the first time in weeks, the room stayed silent at nine o'clock. Karim lay in the quiet dark, proud of his little notebook full of careful clues. He hadn't needed anyone to defeat the "monster." He had needed only a flashlight, a pencil, and the courage to look closely instead of running away.
