Four Percent Battery

The tablet had exactly four percent battery left, and Tariq was determined to finish his game level before it died.

A little English

Past perfect for completed actions before another past action ("had been washed," "had been packed," "had been in the middle of"); present perfect ("dinner has been ready"); natural consequence structure across two full scenes; dialogue expressing calm boundary-setting.

The tablet had exactly four percent battery left, and Tariq was determined to finish his game level before it died. "Tariq, dinner's ready!" his mother called from the kitchen. "One minute!" Tariq shouted back, eyes fixed on the glowing screen. Ten minutes passed. Then fifteen. His mother appeared in the doorway, arms crossed. "Tariq. Dinner has been ready for fifteen minutes. It's getting cold." "I'm almost at the final boss," Tariq mumbled, thumbs moving rapidly across the screen. "Just five more minutes." His mother sighed and walked away without arguing further. Twenty minutes later, Tariq finally looked up, having lost the level anyway when his tablet's battery died at the worst possible moment. He walked to the kitchen, expecting his dinner plate waiting on the table. Instead, he found the kitchen dark and empty. The pots had been washed. The leftovers had been packed into the refrigerator. "Mom?" Tariq called out. His mother appeared from the living room. "Dinner's finished, Tariq. The kitchen closed twenty minutes ago." "But I'm hungry now!" said Tariq, his voice rising with frustration. "I understand," said his mother calmly. "And I called you multiple times. You chose the game over dinner. I'm not upset with you, but I'm also not going to reheat everything just because the choice didn't work out the way you expected." Tariq's stomach growled loudly. "Can I at least have some bread and cheese?" "Of course," said his mother. "That's always available. But tonight's warm dinner, the one we all sat down and enjoyed together as a family, is finished." Tariq sat at the empty table, eating a plain sandwich alone, listening to the quiet hum of the refrigerator. It didn't taste nearly as good as he imagined the real dinner had been. The next evening, when his mother called for dinner, Tariq set his tablet down immediately, even though he had been in the middle of an exciting round. "Wow," said his mother, surprised. "That was fast." "I remembered the sandwich," said Tariq simply, sliding into his usual seat. At the table, his younger sister asked what the special occasion was for tonight's dinner. Their mother had made his favorite dish, chicken and rice with roasted vegetables. "No special occasion," said his mother, smiling. "Just wanted to make something nice since everyone showed up together, on time, for once." Tariq smiled to himself, quietly enjoying the warm meal in a way that felt different from before — not just because the food was good, but because of everyone sitting together, sharing the same table, at the same time. From that evening forward, whenever his mother called for dinner, Tariq learned to pause his game immediately, promising himself he could always return to it later. Dinner, he realized, couldn't wait the same way a video game level could.
