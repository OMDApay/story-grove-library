The Sticky Fingers

A little English

Every time Sami's little cousin Faisal came to visit, he touched everything without asking — Sami's toy cars, his puzzle pieces, even the drawing Sami had spent all afternoon working on. "Stop it!" Sami snapped, snatching his drawing away from Faisal's sticky fingers. "You always ruin everything!" Faisal's lower lip trembled. "I just wanted to see it." "Well, ask first next time!" said Sami, folding the drawing carefully and placing it on a high shelf, out of reach. Faisal wandered off to a corner, looking smaller than usual, and quietly began stacking a few blocks by himself. Sami's grandmother, visiting for the weekend, had watched the entire exchange from her chair. "Sami, come sit with me for a moment." Sami sighed and sat down beside her. "Faisal never asks before touching my things. It's so annoying." "How old is Faisal?" asked his grandmother. "Four," said Sami. "And how old were you when I used to visit your house?" Sami shrugged. "I don't know. Young, I guess." "You were exactly four," said his grandmother, smiling. "And do you know what you used to do whenever I visited?" Sami shook his head. "You touched everything in my bag without asking. My glasses, my medicine bottles, my old photographs. Once, you even tried to eat one of my house keys." Sami laughed despite himself. "I did that?" "Every single visit," said his grandmother. "It wasn't because you were trying to annoy anyone. Four-year-olds are curious about everything, and they haven't learned yet that some things belong to other people in a special way. That understanding comes with time and gentle reminders, not anger." Sami glanced over at Faisal, still quietly stacking blocks alone in the corner, looking a little lonely. "So he's not doing it on purpose?" asked Sami. "Probably not," said his grandmother. "But that doesn't mean you can't set gentle limits. You can say, 'Please ask before you touch my things,' calmly, instead of snapping. He's little. He's still learning the rules that you already know." Sami thought about this for a moment, then walked over to Faisal. "Hey. Want to see my drawing? I'll show you properly this time." Faisal's face lit up instantly. "Yes! Please!" Sami brought the drawing down carefully and held it where Faisal could see every detail without touching it. "This part is a dragon, and this part is its cave." "It's so cool!" said Faisal, eyes wide with admiration, hands staying carefully in his own lap. "Want to build something together instead?" asked Sami, gesturing at the scattered blocks. Faisal nodded eagerly, and the two cousins spent the rest of the afternoon building a tall, wobbly castle together, Faisal asking politely each time before adding a new block to Sami's design. That evening, Sami's grandmother smiled from her chair, watching the boys laugh together. Patience, she thought, really is something that gets passed quietly from one generation to the next.
