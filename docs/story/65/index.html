The Science Fair Project

If Sara had known how much work the science fair project would be, she would have started earlier.

A little English

Third conditional ("if I had started earlier, it would've been easier"); phrasal verbs ("figure out," "look after," "given up"); adverbial clauses of contrast ("although she was exhausted," "even though you started late"); infinitives of purpose ("to spend one hour," "to finish everything," "to succeed"); mixed conditionals connecting past to present.

If Sara had known how much work the science fair project would be, she would have started earlier. But she hadn't known. She'd thought she could figure it out quickly, even though her teacher had warned them to start weeks in advance. Now, with only three days left before the fair, Sara was starting to panic. "I need to build a working volcano," she told her father one evening, looking at the pile of supplies on the kitchen counter. "And I have to write a report, and make a display board, and prepare a presentation." "That's a lot," her father agreed. "But if you break it into smaller pieces, you can figure it out." Sara sighed. "I should've started sooner. If I had, I wouldn't be so stressed right now." "That's true," said her father. "But even though you didn't start early, you can still do good work. You just need to use the time you have wisely." Sara considered this. "So although I made a mistake by waiting, I can still make good choices now?" "Exactly," said her father. "To succeed, you need to focus on what you can control, not what you can't." That evening, Sara made a plan: to spend one hour on the volcano, one hour on the report, and thirty minutes organizing her display board. She would repeat this routine for the next three days, to finish everything before the deadline. "Want to look after the volcano building with me?" she asked her younger brother. "I could use some help mixing the baking soda and vinegar." "Sure," he said. "But I'm not doing your report. That's your job." "Fair enough," said Sara. "We each do what we're good at." By the end of the third day, Sara had completed everything: the volcano worked perfectly, the report was written neatly, and the display board looked professional. Although she was exhausted, she felt proud. She had made a mistake by waiting, but she hadn't given up. The next day at the fair, Sara's volcano erupted beautifully during her presentation, earning applause from the audience. "You did it," said her father, smiling. "Even though you started late, you finished strong." "I did," said Sara. "And I learned something important: if I had started earlier, it would've been easier. But even though it was hard, I'm glad I didn't give up." "That's exactly right," said her father. "To grow, we need to learn from our mistakes without letting them define us." Sara nodded, feeling a quiet sense of accomplishment. She hadn't done everything perfectly. But she had done enough, and that was what mattered.
