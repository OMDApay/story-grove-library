The Hair in the Mirror

Every morning before school, Nadia checked her hair in the mirror at least ten times, and every morning, she found something new to dislike.

A little English

Past perfect for unnoticed backstory ("hadn't considered," "had only ever seen," "had spent"); present perfect in dialogue; reported realization; comparative discussion of appearance; reflective final-scene contrast structure (mirrored beginning/end scene).

Every morning before school, Nadia checked her hair in the mirror at least ten times, and every morning, she found something new to dislike. "My hair is too curly," she muttered, pulling at a stubborn strand. "Everyone else's hair just falls straight and neat." Her older sister, Huda, leaned against the doorway, watching. "Who's everyone?" "Layla, Sarah, that new girl in class," said Nadia, tugging harder. "Their hair looks perfect in every photo." "Have you seen Layla on a windy day?" asked Huda, raising an eyebrow. Nadia paused. "What do you mean?" "I saw her at pickup last week during that huge sandstorm," said Huda. "Her hair was a complete mess, sticking up everywhere. She looked exactly as frustrated as you look right now." Nadia hadn't considered that. She had only ever seen Layla's hair in careful, posed photos, or right after her mother had spent twenty minutes styling it that morning. At school that day, during art class, the teacher assigned partners for a poster project. Nadia ended up paired with Layla herself. "I love your curls," said Layla suddenly, halfway through painting a poster border. "Mine won't do anything interesting. It's just flat and boring, no matter what I try." Nadia blinked in surprise. "You think MY hair is interesting? I spend every morning wishing it looked like yours." Layla laughed, though not unkindly. "That's so strange. I spend every morning wishing mine had some texture, like yours does. It just lies there, doing nothing." They stared at each other for a moment, both slightly stunned. "Wait," said Nadia slowly, "so we've both been wishing for the other person's hair this whole time?" "Apparently," said Layla, grinning now. "That feels kind of silly, now that we're saying it out loud." That afternoon, walking home, Nadia told Huda everything that had happened during art class. "See?" said Huda. "Nobody actually has it all figured out, even if their photos make it look that way. You're only seeing everyone else's best five seconds. You never see their messy mornings, their bad hair days, or their own list of complaints." "So I should just... stop comparing myself to people I don't actually know that well?" asked Nadia. "Not stop noticing what you like about others," said Huda. "Just remember that whatever you're noticing is only a tiny slice of their whole story, not the complete picture." The next morning, Nadia stood in front of the mirror again. Her hair curled the same way it always had. But this time, instead of listing complaints, she tried something different. She twisted two sections back from her face and let the rest fall naturally. She actually liked how it looked. At school, Layla noticed immediately. "Your hair looks great today! What did you do differently?" "Nothing, really," said Nadia, smiling. "I just stopped trying to make it into something it wasn't."
