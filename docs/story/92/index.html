What Makes You Think That?

When Youssef heard that chocolate milk was healthier than water, he believed it immediately.

A little English

Reported speech for multi-turn dialogue ("his mother asked what made him think that," "he explained that he used to believe"); contrast clauses for nuanced thinking ("not about being skeptical of everything... it's about asking good questions"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's right, isn't it?"); present perfect for accumulated realization ("you've learned"); future forms for prediction ("you'll decide").

When Youssef heard that chocolate milk was healthier than water, he believed it immediately. "Everyone at school says it's true," he told his mother. "Chocolate milk has milk, so it's healthy. And it tastes better than water." His mother raised an eyebrow. "That's an interesting claim. What makes you think that's true?" Youssef paused. "Well... everyone says it is." "Okay," said his mother. "But have you checked if that's actually true? Or are you just accepting what everyone says?" Youssef thought about this. "I guess I'm just accepting it." "Let's investigate," suggested his mother. "First question: who is behind this claim? Is it a scientist? A doctor? Or just kids at school?" "Just kids at school," admitted Youssef. "Second question," continued his mother. "What evidence supports this claim? Is there any research? Or is it just an opinion?" Youssef considered this. "I don't think there's any research. It's just... what everyone says." "Third question," said his mother. "What might be missing from this claim? Is there any information that contradicts it?" Youssef thought hard. "Well... chocolate milk has sugar. And too much sugar isn't healthy. So maybe it's not healthier than water after all." "Exactly," said his mother. "You're thinking critically. You're questioning assumptions, looking for evidence, and considering alternative explanations." That evening, Youssef told his father about the conversation. "I used to believe what everyone said," he explained. "But now I know I should ask, 'What makes you think that?'" "That's exactly right," said his father. "Critical thinking isn't about being skeptical of everything. It's about asking good questions before you decide what to believe." Youssef nodded. He had learned something important: not everything people say is true — and asking questions helps him figure out what's real.
