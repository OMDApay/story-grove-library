The If-Then Plan

On Monday morning, Omar's teacher introduced a new strategy.

A little English

First conditional for planning ("If it's 4 PM, then I'll start"); reported speech for teacher's instruction ("Ms. Rania said that if-then plans help"); contrast clauses for nuanced explanation ("because you've already decided"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's right, isn't it?"); present perfect for accumulated success ("you've got two plans working").

On Monday morning, Omar's teacher introduced a new strategy. "Today," said Ms. Rania, "we're going to learn about if-then plans. These are plans that help you reach your goals by deciding in advance what you'll do when a specific situation happens." Omar looked confused. "Like what?" "Like this," said Ms. Rania. "If your goal is to finish your homework before dinner, you could make an if-then plan: 'If it's 4 PM, then I'll start my homework.' Or 'If I get home from school, then I'll do my homework before I play.'" Omar thought about this. "So it's like... deciding ahead of time what I'll do?" "Exactly," said Ms. Rania. "Research shows that if-then plans help people stick to their goals. Because you've already decided what to do, you don't have to think about it in the moment." That evening, Omar told his mother about the strategy. "I want to make an if-then plan for my homework," he explained. "That's wonderful," said his mother. "What's your goal?" "To finish my homework before I play video games," said Omar. "And what's your if-then plan?" asked his mother. Omar thought about it. "If I get home from school, then I'll do my homework first. Before I do anything else." "That's a great plan," said his mother. "Let's write it down and put it on the fridge." The next day, Omar came home from school. He wanted to play video games — but then he remembered his plan. "If I get home from school, then I'll do my homework first," he said to himself. He sat down and started his homework. It was hard, but he kept going. When he finished, he felt proud. That evening, he told his mother, "My if-then plan worked! I did my homework before I played, and I didn't even have to think about it." "That's exactly how if-then plans work," said his mother. "You decide ahead of time, so you don't have to decide in the moment." Omar nodded. "Can I make another if-then plan? For my chores?" "Of course," said his mother. "What's your goal?" "To finish my chores without being reminded," said Omar. "And what's your if-then plan?" Omar smiled. "If it's after dinner, then I'll do my chores before I watch TV." His mother wrote it down. "Perfect. Now you've got two if-then plans working for you." Omar felt proud. He had learned a strategy that would help him reach his goals — not just today, but every day.
