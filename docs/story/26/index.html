The Skibidi Dinner

"That is SO skibidi," Faris announced at the dinner table, grinning at his own plate of spaghetti as if he'd said something brilliant.

A little English

Reported explanation dialogue (heavy back-and-forth Q&A structure); present tense for defining slang ("it means," "it doesn't really mean anything"); past tense nested memory (father's own childhood slang); conditional negotiation structure ("Can we agree on a limit"); reflective closing dialogue between mother and son.

"That is SO skibidi," Faris announced at the dinner table, grinning at his own plate of spaghetti as if he'd said something brilliant. His father set down his fork, completely baffled. "That's so... what?" "Skibidi," Faris repeated, as though the word explained itself. "It means it's crazy. Or maybe cool. Depends." "Depends on what?" asked his father. Faris shrugged. "Just depends. You wouldn't get it." "Try me," said his father, leaning forward with genuine curiosity instead of the usual eye-roll Faris expected. "Okay, so," Faris began, sitting up a little straighter, clearly surprised to be asked instead of told to stop. "There's this show called Skibidi Toilet. It's toilets with heads that sing, fighting robot cameramen. It doesn't really mean anything. That's kind of the whole point." His father blinked. "Toilets with heads. Fighting cameramen. And this is entertaining?" "It's supposed to be weird on purpose," said Faris. "Everyone at school says it. If I don't know the words, I feel kind of left out of the jokes." "So it's less about the toilets," said his father slowly, "and more about being part of something with your friends?" Faris paused, considering this. "I guess so? I never really thought about it that way." "That actually makes sense to me," said his father. "When I was your age, we had our own strange words too. My friends and I said 'radical' about absolutely everything, and my mother had no idea what any of it meant." "Wait, you had brainrot too?" asked Faris, delighted. "We didn't call it that, but probably, yes," his father admitted, laughing. "Every upbringing seems to invent words that make adults feel completely lost. I think that might actually be part of the point." "So you're not going to tell me to stop saying it?" asked Faris cautiously. "I'm not going to pretend to love it," said his father honestly. "But I understand it a little better now. I do want to ask you something, though. How much of your screen time is Skibidi videos and similar stuff?" Faris thought about it. "Probably a lot, if I'm being honest." "Here's my only real rule," said his father. "The words themselves don't bother me much. But I want your brain filled with plenty of other things too — books, building things, actual conversations, not just fifteen-second videos all evening. Can we agree on a limit for that kind of content specifically?" "Like an hour?" Faris offered. "Let's try that," said his father. "And in trade-off, you have to actually teach me one new slang word every week. Deal?" Faris grinned. "Deal. Next week's word is 'rizz.' You're going to need to sit down for that one." His father laughed. "I already regret this deal a little." That night, as Faris got ready for bed, he told his mother about the conversation. "Dad actually asked me to explain it instead of just telling me it was dumb," he said, sounding a little amazed. "That's never happened before." "Sometimes understanding works better than banning," said his mother, smiling. "Now go easy on him with 'rizz.' That one's going to take a while."
