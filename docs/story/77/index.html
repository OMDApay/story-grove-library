The Truth About Lying

When Youssef broke the neighbor's window with his football, he had a choice to make.

A little English

Reported speech for internal dialogue and father's teaching ("his father had taught him that honesty builds trust," "he said he was scared"); future forms for consequence prediction ("Mr. Hassan will be mad," "he'll be even madder"); contrast clauses for nuanced reasoning ("even when it's hard," "even when it's scary"); question tags for reflective discussion ("That's right, isn't it?"); present perfect for accumulated trust ("you've built").

When Youssef broke the neighbor's window with his football, he had a choice to make. He could tell the truth and face the consequences. Or he could lie and hope nobody found out. "If I tell the truth," he told himself, "Mr. Hassan will be mad. But if I lie, and he finds out, he'll be even madder." He thought about what his father had taught him: "Honesty builds trust. Every time you tell the truth, even when it's hard, you build trust with the people around you. But every time you lie, you break that trust — and it takes a long time to build it back." Youssef took a deep breath and knocked on Mr. Hassan's door. "Mr. Hassan," he said, "I broke your window. I was playing football, and it flew over the fence. I'm really sorry." Mr. Hassan looked at the broken window, then at Youssef. "Thank you for telling me the truth, Youssef. I am upset about the window. But I'm proud of you for being honest." Youssef felt relief wash over him. "I can help pay for it," he offered. "I've been saving my allowance." "That's very responsible," said Mr. Hassan. "Let's talk to your parents about the best way to fix this." That evening, Youssef told his father what had happened. "I was scared to tell the truth," he admitted. "But I remembered what you said about trust." His father pulled him into a hug. "I know it was hard. But you chose honesty even when it was scary. That's called integrity — doing the right thing even when nobody's watching, or even when it's hard." Youssef nodded. "It still feels a little bad. But also... kind of good? Like I did the right thing." "That's exactly how integrity feels," said his father. "It doesn't always feel easy. But it always feels right." The next week, Mr. Hassan thanked Youssef again for being honest. "You know," he said, "I trust you more now than I did before the window broke. Because I know you'll tell me the truth, even when it's hard." Youssef felt proud. The window was fixed. But the trust he had built — that was worth more than any window.
