My Body, My Rules

During PE class, the teacher announced a partner stretching exercise.

A little English

Second conditional for hypothetical scenarios ("what if someone gets mad?"); modal verbs for permission and ability (can, could, get to); present simple for stating personal rules ("my body belongs to me"); reported advice dialogue with firm boundary language; question tags for confirmation ("That's okay, isn't it?").

During PE class, the teacher announced a partner stretching exercise. "Find someone to stretch with. You'll help each other reach further." Layla paired up with her friend Mona, but when they started, Mona reached out to grab Layla's shoulders to push her forward. "Wait," said Layla, stepping back slightly. "Can you not touch my shoulders? I'd rather you just tell me where to move instead." Mona looked confused. "Why? I'm just trying to help." "I know," said Layla. "But I don't really like being touched during stretching, even by friends. It makes me feel conflicted." "Oh," said Mona, pulling her hands back. "I didn't know that. Is that okay?" "Yeah," said Layla. "It's okay for different people to have different rules about their own bodies. My body belongs to me, and your body belongs to you. We both get to decide what feels okay for us." Their PE teacher, Ms. Rania, had been listening nearby. "Layla's absolutely right," she said, walking over. "Everyone's body is their own. You always get to say what kind of touch feels okay to you, and what doesn't." "But what if someone gets mad?" asked Mona. "What if they think I'm being mean by saying no?" "Then they're not respecting your boundary," said Ms. Rania. "And that's actually important information about them, not about you. A good friend respects your 'no' even if they don't fully understand it." After class, Layla told her older sister about the conversation. "I felt kind of weird saying no to Mona," she admitted. "She was just trying to help." "How did she respond?" asked her sister. "She was okay with it," said Layla. "She asked if it was okay, and I said yes, it's okay for me to have my own rules." "That's exactly right," said her sister. "Your body belongs to you. That means you get to decide who touches you, when, and how. Even if someone means well, you still get to say what feels comfortable." "What if someone doesn't listen?" asked Layla. "Then you tell a trusted adult," said her sister steadfastly. "A teacher, a parent, anyone you trust. Your safety matters more than worrying about being polite." The following week, during a group game, another classmate tried to pull Layla into a group hug without asking first. "Wait," said Layla, stepping back. "I don't want a hug right now. You can ask first next time." The classmate looked surprised but nodded. "Okay, sorry. I'll ask." That evening, Layla told her sister what had happened. "It felt easier the second time," she said. "Like I was allowed to say what I wanted." "You are allowed," said her sister. "Always. Your body, your rules. That's not something you ever have to apologize for."
