What Is Justice?

During dinner, Sara's brother asked a big question.

A little English

Complex sentences for nuanced definition ("Justice is about fairness, but it's not just about..."); reported speech for multi-turn dialogue ("her father explained that justice requires courage"); contrast clauses for conceptual distinction ("not just about everyone getting the same thing — it's about everyone getting what they need"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's right, isn't it?"); present perfect for accumulated understanding ("you've learned").

During dinner, Sara's brother asked a big question. "What is justice?" he said. "My teacher talked about it today, but I don't really get it." Sara's parents looked at each other. This was a conversation they'd been waiting for. "Justice," said her father, "is about fairness. But it's not just about everyone getting the same thing — it's about everyone getting what they need to be treated fairly." Sara thought about this. "Like... if someone is hungry, justice means giving them food?" "Exactly," said her mother. "Justice is also about protecting people's rights — like the right to be safe, the right to go to school, and the right to be treated with respect." Sara considered this. "So justice is like... making sure everyone is treated fairly?" "Yes," said her father. "And sometimes, justice requires courage — the willingness to stand up for what's right, even when it's hard." Sara thought about this. "Like if someone is being bullied, and you stand up for them even though you're scared?" "Exactly," said her mother. "That takes courage. And it's an act of justice." Sara's brother nodded. "So justice, fairness, rights, and courage are all connected?" "They are," said her father. "And there's another important word: integrity. Integrity means doing the right thing even when nobody's watching. It's how you show justice and courage in your daily life." Sara smiled. "So if I see someone being treated unfairly, and I speak up even though it's scary, that's integrity?" "That's exactly right," said her mother. "You're using these abstract concepts correctly. Justice, fairness, rights, courage, integrity — these are big ideas that guide how we treat each other." That evening, Sara told her father what she had learned. "I used to think justice was just a word," she explained. "But now I see it's something you do." "That's exactly right," said her father. "Abstract concepts aren't just ideas — they're guides for action. And you're already living them." Sara felt proud. She had learned something important: big words like justice, freedom, responsibility, courage, and integrity aren't just vocabulary — they're values that shape who you become.
