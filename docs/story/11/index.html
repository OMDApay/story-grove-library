The Corn on the Corner

Ramy had been saving his allowance for six weeks, and finally, he had enough for the remote-control car he had wanted since his birthday.

A little English

Past perfect for prior saving ("had been saving," "had wanted"); conditional reasoning about consequences; comparative structure ("needed it more than"); reported internal thought; multi-scene time sequencing (three days later, by the weekend).

Ramy had been saving his allowance for six weeks, and finally, he had enough for the remote-control car he had wanted since his birthday. He counted the coins one final time on his bedroom floor. Forty-five riyals exactly. The toy cost forty-five riyals. Not a single coin to spare. His mother knocked and peeked in. "Ready to go to the shop?" "Ready!" said Ramy, sweeping the coins into a small pouch. On the way, they passed old Mr. Fahad, who sold roasted corn from a small cart on the corner. Business looked slow; only a few ears of corn remained, and Mr. Fahad sat quietly, fanning himself in the afternoon heat. "Assalamu alaikum, Ramy," said Mr. Fahad, smiling despite the heat. "Off somewhere exciting?" "I'm buying a remote-control car!" said Ramy proudly. "I saved for six whole weeks." "That's wonderful patience for someone your age," said Mr. Fahad warmly. They continued walking. A block later, Ramy noticed a small girl sitting on the curb near the corn cart, her knees pulled up to her chest. It was Hanan, a girl from his building whose father had been out of work for months. "My little brother wants corn so badly," Hanan said quietly to no one in particular, mostly to herself. "But we don't have enough money today." Ramy stopped walking. He glanced at the pouch in his hand, then at Hanan, then at the toy shop still two blocks away. "Mom," he whispered, "can we wait one minute?" Ramy walked over to Mr. Fahad's cart. "How much for two ears of corn?" "Six riyals," said Mr. Fahad. Ramy's stomach twisted. Six riyals meant he would only have thirty-nine left. Not enough for the car. Not even close. He thought about the remote-control car he had dreamed about for six weeks. Then he thought about Hanan's little brother, probably hungry, waiting somewhere nearby. "I'll take two, please," Ramy said, before he could change his mind. He counted out six riyals and walked over to Hanan, holding out two warm ears of roasted corn. "Here. For you and your brother." Hanan's eyes widened. "Are you sure? Don't you need that money for something?" "It's fine," said Ramy, though his voice wavered slightly. "I can wait a little longer for my car." Hanan thanked him three times before running off with the corn, calling for her brother. Ramy's mother had watched the whole exchange silently. As they continued walking, she finally spoke. "You know you won't have enough for the car today." "I know," said Ramy, feeling a strange mixture of disappointment and something warmer underneath it. "But Hanan needed it more than I needed the car right now." At the toy shop, Ramy stood outside the window, looking at the car he couldn't afford, and felt surprisingly okay about it. Three days later, Ramy's grandmother visited and, without knowing anything about the corn, handed him an envelope. "I heard from your mother about what you did for that little girl on the street. This is for being the kind of person I hope you always are." Inside was exactly six riyals. Ramy laughed out loud, understanding immediately. He added it to his savings, and by the weekend, the remote-control car was finally his — along with something else that felt even better than the toy itself.
