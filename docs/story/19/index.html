Lost Near the Dollhouse

A little English

Past continuous for the drifting sequence ("was wandering," "was following"); present continuous quoted as internal thought ("Just to look, I'll come right back"); past perfect continuous ("had been searching frantically"); dialogue modeling calm post-incident reflection rather than punishment; conditional safety instruction ("if you're ever lost...").

"Wait for me at the entrance. Don't wander off into the mall by yourself," Layla's father said firmly before heading into the pharmacy. "I'll only be five minutes." Layla nodded, standing by the entrance exactly as told. But five minutes felt very long when a toy shop window, glittering with a giant dollhouse display, sat only a short distance away. Just to look, Layla told herself. I'll come right back. She wandered toward the toy shop window, then a little further to see the dollhouse from a different angle, then further still, following the mall's curve around a corner, completely losing sight of the pharmacy entrance. When she turned to head back, nothing looked familiar. The pharmacy was gone. The entrance was gone. Rows of unfamiliar shops stretched in every direction, and hundreds of strangers moved past her in a blur. Layla's heart began pounding. "Baba?" she called out, her voice small against the noise of the crowded mall. Nobody answered. She spun in one direction, then another, panic rising in her chest like water filling a cup. Tears blurred her vision. She had never felt so completely alone in a crowd before. Remember what Baba taught you, a small voice in her head reminded her, cutting through the panic. Find a helper, not just any stranger. Layla remembered the safety lesson: if you're ever lost, look for someone who works there — a shop worker with a uniform, a security guard, or someone standing behind a counter — not just any adult walking by. She spotted a woman wearing a mall uniform standing near an information desk and ran toward her, tears now streaming freely. "I lost my dad," Layla said between sobs. "I was supposed to wait by the pharmacy, but I walked away, and now I can't find it." The uniformed woman knelt down immediately. "You're safe now, sweetheart. What's your dad's name? We'll call an announcement over the speakers right now." Within two minutes, Layla's name echoed through the entire mall's speaker system. Her father, who had been searching frantically near the pharmacy entrance for the past ten minutes, heard it immediately and rushed toward the information desk. "Layla!" he called out, relief flooding his face as he scooped her into a tight hug. "I'm so sorry, Baba," Layla cried into his shoulder. "You told me to wait, and I didn't listen. I just wanted to see the dollhouse for one minute." Her father held her tightly for a long moment before speaking. "I was so scared, Layla. That's exactly why I asked you to wait in one place — not because I don't trust you to look at toy shops, but because it's incredibly easy to get lost in a place this big, this fast." "I understand now," said Layla, wiping her eyes. "I really do. It felt like forever, being lost." "I know," said her father gently. "And I'm proud of you for remembering to find a helper instead of panicking completely, or worse, going with just anyone who offered to help. That part, you did exactly right." On the drive home, Layla stayed unusually quiet, thinking about the rows of unfamiliar shops and the sound of her own name over the loudspeakers. "Next time," she said finally, "I'll wait exactly where you tell me. Even if something really interesting is right there." "That," said her father, smiling with relief, "is exactly the lesson I hoped you'd learn — just hopefully not quite so dramatically next time."
