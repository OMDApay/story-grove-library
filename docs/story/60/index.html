To Show I Care

Sara woke up early on Saturday morning to clean her room before her friends came over.

A little English

Infinitives of purpose used systematically throughout as the central grammar teaching point ("to clean," "to make sure," "to pick up," "to show," "to create"); reported dialogue with natural purpose-explanation; adverbial clauses of contrast for nuanced comparison ("whereas the party itself had been fun"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's smart, isn't it?").

Sara woke up early on Saturday morning to clean her room before her friends came over. "Why are you cleaning now?" asked her younger brother, watching her make the bed. "Mom said we don't have to do chores until after lunch." "I know," said Sara. "But I'm cleaning now to make sure everything's ready when my friends arrive. I don't want to spend the whole afternoon picking up instead of hanging out with them." Her brother nodded, as if this made sense. "That's actually pretty sensible." Later that morning, Sara's mother asked her to go to the grocery store to pick up snacks for the party. "I can go," offered Sara. "I need to get some air anyway, and I want to make sure we have enough chips and juice for everyone." "Are you sure?" asked her mother. "You've already cleaned your room. You don't have to do everything." "I want to," said Sara. "To be honest, I'm kind of excited about this party. I haven't seen these friends in a while, and I want everything to go smoothly." Her mother smiled. "Okay, then. Thank you for helping." At the store, Sara carefully chose snacks to please different tastes: chips for Ahmed, who loved salty things; fruit snacks for Layla, who preferred healthier options; and juice boxes for everyone, because nobody ever said no to those. When she got home, she arranged everything neatly on the kitchen counter, to make serving easy later. "You're really going all out for this," her mother observed, watching her set up. "I am," said Sara. "To tell the truth, I was nervous about this party. These friends are important to me, and I want them to have fun. So I'm doing all this to show them I care." Her mother considered this. "You know, they already know you care. You don't have to do everything perfectly to prove that." "I know," said Sara. "But doing these things makes me feel better, too. To have a plan, to prepare, to know I've done what I can — that helps me relax." When her friends arrived, they were delighted by the setup. "You did all this for us?" asked Layla, looking at the snack spread. "I did," said Sara. "To make sure we could just hang out and not worry about anything else." "That's so thoughtful," said Ahmed. "We're lucky to have you as a friend." Sara felt a warm glow of satisfaction. She had done all this to create a good experience for her friends, and it had worked. That evening, after the party was over and everyone had gone home, Sara's mother found her in the kitchen, starting to clean up the snack mess. "Need help?" she asked. "Sure," said Sara. "But honestly, I'm not even tired. To spend the afternoon with my friends like that — it was worth all the preparation." Her mother smiled. "That's exactly right. When we do things to show people we care, it usually comes back to us in good ways." Sara nodded, feeling fulfilled. She had cleaned to prepare, she had shopped to provide, and she had planned to create a space where her friends felt welcomed. And whereas the party itself had been fun, the knowledge that she had done all this to show love — that felt even better.
