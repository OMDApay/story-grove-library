What They Said About the Project

After the group project presentations, Omar told his father about what his teammates had said.

A little English

Reported speech used systematically with backshift ("Sara said that she had enjoyed," "he said that he would design," "she said that our team had won"); relative clauses used systematically ("the details which she added," "the power grid which he planned," "the team which had collaborated"); future forms for plans and predictions ("I'm going to keep practicing," "it will help me"); contrast clauses for conceptual distinction ("not just rules — they're tools"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's right, isn't it?").

After the group project presentations, Omar told his father about what his teammates had said. "Sara said that she had enjoyed painting the buildings," Omar reported. "And she said that the details which she added made the model look realistic." His father smiled. "You're using reported speech and relative clauses correctly," he observed. "You said 'Sara said that she had enjoyed' — that's reported speech with backshift. And you said 'the details which she added' — that's a relative clause describing the details." Omar nodded. "Youssef said that he would design the roads tomorrow. And he said that the power grid which he planned would be solar-powered." "Perfect," said his father. "You said 'he would design' — that's reported speech with future-in-the-past. And 'the power grid which he planned' — that's another relative clause." That evening, Adam told his mother about the project too. "Ms. Rania said that the team which had collaborated best would win," he reported. "And she said that our team had won because we had divided tasks by strength." "Excellent," said his mother. "You're using reported speech with past perfect ('she had won,' 'we had divided') and relative clauses ('the team which had collaborated') correctly. That's advanced grammar." Adam smiled. "I'm going to keep practicing," he said. "Because I think it will help me communicate more clearly." "That's exactly right," said his mother. "Reported speech helps you communicate what others have said accurately. Relative clauses help you describe people and things precisely. And future forms help you talk about plans and predictions. You're using all three structures naturally now." Adam felt proud. He hadn't realized he was using three grammar structures at once — but now that he knew, he noticed them everywhere.
