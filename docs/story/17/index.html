The Safe Word

Omar was waiting outside his school gate for his mother, kicking small stones near the fence, when a man he had never seen before walked up smiling.

A little English

Past tense narration throughout; conditional structure for safety rules ("If someone says... they have to know"); reported speech; sequencing of a real-time tense scene; dialogue expressing calm assertiveness under pressure.

Omar was waiting outside his school gate for his mother, kicking small stones near the fence, when a man he had never seen before walked up smiling. "Hey there! Omar, right?" said the man. "Your mom asked me to pick you up today. She's stuck at work and sent me instead. I have candy in my car if you're hungry." Omar's stomach did a strange little flip. He had learned about this exact situation during a school safety lesson just last month. "How do you know my name?" Omar asked carefully, staying exactly where he was, near the school gate where his teacher, Mr. Adel, stood nearby watching students. "Your mom told me, of course," said the man, still smiling. "Come on, she's waiting." "What's the safe word?" asked Omar. The man paused. "The... what?" "My family has a safe word," said Omar, his voice steady even though his heart was beating fast. "If someone says my mom sent them, they have to know our safe word. If you really know my mom, you'll know it." The man's smile faltered slightly. "I, uh... I don't remember exactly. Let's just go, we're wasting time." Omar took two steps backward, closer to his teacher instead of the stranger. "I'm going to go get my teacher now." "Wait—" the man started, but Omar had already turned and walked quickly toward Mr. Adel. "Mr. Adel," said Omar, "that man over there says my mom sent him to pick me up, but he doesn't know our safe word." Mr. Adel's expression changed immediately to serious attention. He walked toward the man, but by the time he reached the gate, the man had already hurried away down the street and disappeared around the corner. Mr. Adel immediately called the school office, and the school called Omar's mother right away. She arrived twenty minutes later, worried and out of breath, having rushed straight from work. "Omar, are you alright?" she asked, hugging him tightly. "I'm okay," said Omar. "I remembered the safe word rule, just like we practiced." His mother knelt down to his eye level. "I am so, so proud of you. You did exactly the right thing. You didn't go anywhere with him, you asked the safe word question, and you went straight to a trusted adult. That's exactly what to do, every single time." "He got scared when I asked about the safe word," said Omar. "Does that mean he really wasn't sent by you?" "It means you were smart enough to check," said his mother. "Real friends or family members I send will always know our safe word, without hesitating. Anyone who doesn't know it, or who tries to rush you, or offers treats to get you to come quickly — those are all signs that something isn't right." That evening, Omar's family sat together and reviewed their safety rules once more: never go anywhere with someone you don't know well, even if they know your name; always ask for the safe word if someone claims a parent sent them; and always, immediately, tell a trusted adult like a teacher, parent, or police officer if anything feels wrong. "The safe word worked," said Omar's father, "because you actually used it, calmly, exactly like we practiced. That's the important part — not just knowing the rule, but actually using it when it matters." Omar nodded, feeling braver than he had that afternoon. He understood now that the strange little flip in his stomach earlier had been an important signal, one worth listening to every time.
