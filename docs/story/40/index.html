The Volcano Project

Mira's two best friends, Salma and Ward, had stopped speaking to each other entirely, and Mira found herself stuck uncomfortably in the middle.

Mira's two best friends, Salma and Ward, had stopped speaking to each other entirely, and Mira found herself stuck uncomfortably in the middle. "Salma said I copied her project idea," Ward complained during recess, arms crossed tightly. "I didn't even know what she was doing. I came up with mine completely on my own." Later that same day, Salma cornered Mira by the water fountain. "Ward definitely copied me. I told her about my volcano idea last week, and now suddenly hers is a volcano too? That's not a coincidence." Mira felt torn in two directions. Both friends seemed genuinely convinced they were right, and both seemed equally hurt. "I don't know what to do," Mira told her mother that evening. "If I agree with Salma, Ward will think I'm taking sides against her. If I agree with Ward, Salma will feel like nobody believes her." "What if you didn't take either side?" suggested her mother. "Is that even possible?" asked Mira. "They both want me to agree with them." "You could try asking questions instead of picking a side," said her mother. "Sometimes conflicts happen because two people genuinely experienced something differently, not because one of them is lying." The next day, Mira brought both girls together during lunch, her heart pounding nervously. "Can we all talk about this together instead of separately? I think there might be a misunderstanding." Salma crossed her arms. "There's no misunderstanding. She copied me." "Ward," said Mira carefully, "did you know Salma was planning a volcano project before you chose yours?" Ward paused, actually thinking instead of immediately defending herself. "I... I might have heard her mention something about lava at some point. I honestly didn't think about it when I picked mine. I just thought volcanoes seemed cool for the science fair." "Salma," Mira continued, "do you think Ward planned this on purpose, or is it possible she just forgot she'd heard you mention it?" Salma considered this, her expression relaxing slightly. "I guess... I don't actually know for certain that she did it on purpose. I just assumed the worst because I was excited about my idea and felt like it got taken." "Could there be a way to make both projects clearly different from each other?" Mira asked. "So neither of you feels like the other copied?" Ward brightened slightly. "I could focus mine on underwater volcanoes instead. That would actually be really different." "That sounds interesting," Salma admitted, some of the tension leaving her shoulders. "Mine's about volcanoes that erupted in history. They'd barely look similar at all." By the end of lunch, both girls were sketching rough ideas for their now clearly distinct projects, occasionally asking Mira's opinion on details. That evening, Mira told her mother how it had gone. "Nobody was really lying," she said. "Ward genuinely forgot, and Salma genuinely felt hurt. Once they actually talked instead of just being upset separately, it wasn't even that complicated." "That's the thing about most conflicts between friends," said her mother. "They often look impossible from the outside, but they usually just need someone willing to ask calm questions instead of picking a winner." "I didn't even have to choose a side," said Mira, sounding a little astounded herself. "I just had to help them actually talk to each other."
