The Old Construction Site

A little English

Past continuous for the unfolding accident scene; past perfect for backstory warnings ("had warned," "had begun climbing"); imperative dialogue in a crisis ("Don't move, okay?"); reported internal conflict ("the small warning voice in his head"); resolution dialogue distinguishing fear from wisdom.

"Let's go check out the old construction site behind the field," said Khalid's friend Marwan, pointing toward a half-built building surrounded by a rusty fence, abandoned for over a year. "I heard there's a whole maze of rooms in there." Khalid hesitated. "My mom said never to go past the football field without telling her first." "We'll be back before anyone even notices," said Marwan, already climbing through a gap in the rusty fence. "Come on, it'll be an adventure." Against the small warning voice in his head, Khalid squeezed through the gap after his friend. Inside, the building felt different from how it looked from outside — darker, quieter, and strangely uneven under his sneakers. Loose wires hung from the ceiling in some rooms. Piles of broken concrete and rusty metal rods lay scattered across the floor, some sticking up at odd angles. "This is so cool," said Marwan, running ahead toward a staircase missing several steps. "Race you to the top floor!" "Marwan, wait—" Khalid started, but his friend had already begun climbing. CRACK. A weak wooden plank near the top of the staircase gave way under Marwan's foot. He yelped, grabbing desperately at the railing as his leg dropped through the gap, scraping hard against jagged wood and metal. "Khalid! Help! My leg!" Marwan shouted, pain sharp in his voice. Khalid's heart pounded as he rushed over carefully, testing each step before putting his full weight down. He found Marwan stuck, his leg wedged between broken boards, a nasty scrape bleeding along his shin. "I can't pull it out," Marwan said, panic rising in his voice. "It hurts so much." Khalid knew he couldn't safely free his friend's leg alone, and pulling incorrectly might make the injury worse. "I'm going to run and get help. Don't move, okay? I'll be right back." Khalid sprinted out through the fence gap and back across the football field toward the nearest houses, lungs burning, until he reached his own front door. "Mom! Marwan's hurt! At the old construction site! His leg is stuck!" Khalid gasped out between breaths. His mother's face went pale, but she moved fast, calling for help while grabbing her keys and running with Khalid back toward the site. Within minutes, Marwan's parents and a small group of neighbors arrived too, carefully freeing his leg and getting him safely to the hospital, where doctors confirmed a bad scrape and a mild sprain, but nothing broken. That evening, sitting in his room, Khalid felt sick thinking about how much worse things could have gone. His mother sat beside him. "I'm not going to pretend I'm not upset that you went there, Khalid. That site is exactly the kind of place I've warned you about — old buildings, construction sites, empty lots. They look like adventures, but they're full of dangers you can't always see: weak floors, sharp metal, unstable walls." "I knew I shouldn't have gone," Khalid admitted. "I heard your voice in my head the whole time, telling me not to." "Why didn't you listen to it?" asked his mother gently. "Marwan made it sound exciting, and I didn't want to seem scared," said Khalid quietly. "There's a difference between being scared and being smart," said his mother. "The bravest thing you did today wasn't going into that building. It was running for help the second things went wrong, instead of panicking or trying to handle it alone. That part, you did exactly right." The next day, Marwan called Khalid from home, his leg wrapped in a bandage. "Thanks for running for help," he said. "That was scary. I don't think I'll be exploring any more abandoned places for a while." "Me neither," said Khalid firmly. "Next time, if I get that little warning feeling, I'm going to actually listen to it."
