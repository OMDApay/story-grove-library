The Essay That Was Written

Layla was proud of the essay she had written.

A little English

Passive voice used systematically ("the essay was graded," "it was praised"); gerunds and infinitives used systematically ("enjoy writing," "decided to study"); discourse markers used systematically ("however," "therefore," "furthermore"); relative clauses for description ("the feedback which I received"); future forms for plans ("I'm going to apply"); contrast clauses for conceptual distinction ("not just rules — they're tools"); question tags for gentle confirmation ("That's right, isn't it?").

Layla was proud of the essay she had written. "The essay was graded yesterday," she told her father. "And it was praised by Ms. Rania." His father raised an eyebrow. "You're using passive voice," he observed. "You said 'the essay was graded' and 'it was praised' — focusing on the action, not on who did it." Layla nodded. "I also used gerunds and infinitives. I wrote, 'I enjoy writing essays,' and 'I decided to study harder.' And I used discourse markers too: 'However, writing is challenging.' 'Therefore, I practice daily.' 'Furthermore, I seek feedback.'" His father smiled. "You're using all three structures correctly," he said. "Passive voice for focus on the action ('was graded,' 'was praised'). Gerunds after 'enjoy' ('enjoy writing'). Infinitives after 'decided' ('decided to study'). And discourse markers for structured thinking ('however,' 'therefore,' 'furthermore')." "That's a lot," said Layla. "It is," agreed her father. "But you're using them naturally in your speech and writing. You just didn't know the grammar names for them." That evening, Layla told her mother about the essay. "The feedback which I received was helpful," she reported. "And I'm going to apply it to my next essay." "Perfect," said her mother. "You said 'the feedback which I received' — that's a relative clause. And 'I'm going to apply' — that's future with 'going to' for a plan. You're using all the grammar structures we've learned — passive voice, gerunds and infinitives, discourse markers, relative clauses, and future forms — correctly and naturally." Layla felt proud. She had learned something important: grammar isn't just rules — it's tools for clear, precise communication. And she was using those tools skillfully.
