Three More Tries

When Youssef's math problem didn't work out the first time, he wanted to give up.

A little English

When Youssef's math problem didn't work out the first time, he wanted to give up. "This is too hard," he said, pushing the paper away. "I can't do it." His mother, who had been watching from the kitchen, came over and sat beside him. "Youssef," she said gently, "I know this feels hard. But hard doesn't mean impossible. It just means you haven't figured it out yet." Youssef sighed. "But I've already tried once. And it didn't work." "That's okay," said his mother. "Most things don't work the first time. That's why we have a rule in this house: three more tries." "Three more tries?" asked Youssef. "Yes," said his mother. "When you feel like giving up, you try three more times before you decide to stop. Because often, the breakthrough happens on the third or fourth try — right when you feel like quitting." Youssef looked at the math problem. "But what if I try three more times and it still doesn't work?" "Then we'll figure it out together," said his mother. "But I want you to give yourself a chance to figure it out first." Youssef took a deep breath. He tried the problem again. It didn't work. He tried it a second time. Still wrong. He tried it a third time — and this time, it worked. "I did it!" he said, surprised. His mother smiled. "You did. And you know what? You were about to give up right before it clicked. That's how it usually works — the hardest part is right before you figure it out." Youssef nodded. "So when I feel like quitting..." "That's when you know you're close," said his mother. "That's when you try three more times." That evening, Youssef told his father about the math problem. "I wanted to give up," he admitted. "But Mom said to try three more times. And it worked on the third try." His father pulled him into a hug. "That's called perseverance," he said. "Not giving up when things get hard. And you know what? Perseverance is a skill you can practice, just like math." Youssef smiled. He had learned something important: hard doesn't mean impossible. It just means he needed three more tries.
